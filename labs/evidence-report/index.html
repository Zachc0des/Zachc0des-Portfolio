Analysis

  • Single privileged target. Every attempt used root. No other usernames were tried, so this looks like a focused attempt on the highest-value account rather than a broad username sweep.
  • Intermittent, low-rate pattern. Attempts come in short bursts of 3 or 4 (fastest gap 1.7 s) separated by quiet periods of minutes. That is slower than typical unthrottled brute-force tooling and fits manual retries, a throttled script, or a tool with built-in delays.
  • Internal source. 192.168.128.4 is a private (RFC 1918) address, so the activity came from inside the network or from a host that can reach it.
Not shown in this screenshot: whether each attempt failed or succeeded, the destination port, and the authentication method. Confirm in the raw log before drawing conclusions.

Definitions

Source IP: the address the login attempts came from.

Attempt: one logged try to authenticate over SSH.

First seen / Last seen: earliest and latest attempt from this source.

Timestamp: ISO 8601 with microseconds. The -05:00 offset means US Eastern Standard Time.

Burst: a group of attempts within roughly 30 seconds of each other, as used in this report.

root: the all-powerful administrator account on Linux. Logging in as root over SSH is a common attack target.

Suggested follow-up

  • Check the raw auth log for the outcome of each attempt, and for any accepted login after 08:40:59.
  • Identify the device at 192.168.128.4 (DHCP lease, ARP table) and confirm whether it is authorized to connect.
  • If root login is not needed, set PermitRootLogin no, require key-based authentication, and add rate limiting or a tool such as fail2ban.
